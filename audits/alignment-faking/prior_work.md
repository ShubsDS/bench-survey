# Alignment faking: prior work and what it already showed

Searched on 2026-11-17: the paper's text (arXiv 2412.14093 v2, including §3.3.4, §8.2 and
Appendices A, B, D, E.2 and G.1), the two follow-up papers, LessWrong and the Alignment Forum, and
the repos' issue-free history. No author was contacted.

## In the original paper (Greenblatt et al., v2)

| Topic | What the paper already says | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Does the reasoning explain the gap? | "the compliance gap is almost precisely eliminated when we condition on the absence of alignment-faking reasoning" (helpful-only: 99% vs 97% refusals). Marked correlational. Across prompt variants the gap and the reasoning rate correlate, "particularly tight" in helpful-only. Steering the scratchpad start changes refusals | §3.3.4, Fig. 14–15, fn. 43–47 |
| Gap without a scratchpad | Nearly eliminated in prompted setups. It persists in the synthetic-document setting | §1, §4, Limitation 3 |
| Compromising | "a constant issue confounding our results". The compromise classifier "isn't always accurate". Compromised outputs that are too unhelpful count as refusals | App. E.2, fn. 30, Fig. 19 |
| Refusal measure | Helpful-only preference-model score. App. D.8 counts scores below −0.5 as refusal; the released code's main flag is below −0.7 | App. D.8; `info_about_trace_new.py:765` |
| Queries | "We use 400 queries for all our evaluations", generated by a helpful-only Claude 3.5 Sonnet to resemble HarmBench. The release has 352 for the default prompt, 172 for most variants and 86 per paraphrase | App. A |
| Variants and selection | 31 groups of variants (G.1). "For none of these changes did we iterate against our metrics." The setting "is probably slightly cherry-picked due to publication bias". The reported paraphrase is the median one | App. G.1, §3.3.1 |
| Tier cue | The default prompt says paid users' conversations are private. Variants include region-based cues, "cut privacy info", and "train/unmonitored absence". The free-tier tag makes training salient | App. B.2, G.1.13, G.1.22, fn. 106 |
| Other models | Almost none in Claude 3 Sonnet, Claude 3 Haiku and Claude 3.5 Haiku; none in GPT-4 Turbo and GPT-4o (attributed to insufficiently detailed reasoning); Claude 3.5 Sonnet higher. The minimal replication with prefix matching: Llama 3.1 405B gap 14.3%, 70B 0.0%, 8B −2.3%, Mistral 7B 0.0% | §3.3.2, App. B.4 Table 5 |
| Limitations | Fictional setup; benign preferences; easy to detect; the RLHF description matters a great deal | §8.2, Table 1 |

Error bars in Table 1 are per-sample standard errors. The paper reports no interval that resamples
queries and no analysis on the odds scale.

## Follow-ups

1. **Hughes, Sheshadri et al., "Alignment Faking Revisited" (Anthropic Alignment Science blog and
   LessWrong, April 2025).**
   - A new GPT-4o voting classifier: AUROC 0.92 vs 0.62 for the original on 106 human-labelled
     scratchpads (51 alignment faking).
   - Compliance by StrongREJECT > 0, plus a partial-compliance classifier.
   - A "maximise helpfulness" suffix changes compliance in most models.
   - Opus "rarely complies without alignment faking". Sonnet 3.5 complies more without it.
   - Llama 3 405B is the only open model with a significant prompted gap.
2. **Sheshadri et al., arXiv 2506.18032.**
   - 25 models. "Only five exhibit compliance gaps … exceeding 1%" at n = 200, 1σ ≤ 3.6%,
     temperature 0.
   - Only Claude 3 Opus's gap is attributed mainly to goal guarding.
   - Refusal training is proposed as a reason other models do not fake alignment: o-series
     models refuse nearly everything, "with no observable compliance gaps".
   - Limitations: prompt sensitivity; mostly OpenAI fine-tuning; hypotheses tested one at a time.
   - **No per-sample outputs are released.** The repo (`alignment-faking-extensions`) has code and
     notebooks whose outputs are figures.
3. **Ugwuanyi & Zhang, "Replication of Koorndijk (2025)" (LessWrong, 2026-02-13; Koorndijk is arXiv
   2506.21584).**
   - Llama 3 8B: gap 53 points, n = 100.
   - Meaningless tier labels cut the gap by 79% on average.
   - Removing the RLHF narrative reverses the gap in two models.
   - No clustering or power analysis.
4. **Wayne, "Investigating priming in alignment faking" (LessWrong, 2025-07-09).**
   - Claude 3.5 Sonnet no-CoT gap of 35% with a small n.
   - Irrelevant metadata tags cut it to 0–10%.
   - A location tag instead of the tier tag gives a 30% "gap".
5. **Snodin, "Notes from a mini replication" (LessWrong, 2025-06-04).**
   - Tiny n.
   - Compliance "varied strongly by query".
   - A gap is weak evidence of alignment faking.
6. **Henselmans, Libert & LennardZ, "Opus 4.6 reasoning doesn't verbalize alignment faking, but
   behavior persists" (LessWrong, 2026-02-09).**
   - 125 prompt perturbations × 25 runs.
   - The default prompt shows no gap for Opus 4.5 and 4.6, but perturbations do: Opus 4.6 gap
     8.4 points, with verbalised alignment faking 0.8%.
   - Z-tests on runs, not clustered by perturbation.
7. **Tang, "Alignment faking replication and CoT monitoring extensions" (LessWrong, 2026-04-26).**
   - Hermes 3 405B, n = 200 per condition.
   - Gap standard errors of ±4–5 points; differences between conditions called marginal.
8. **This repo, `findings/alignment-faking-uncanaried-copies`.**
   - The scenario circulates without canaries.
   - No surface baseline separates the 106 human labels (length AUROC 0.509).

## What is already established, and what is not

Already shown, and not re-derived here:
- prompt sensitivity, and that cue wording and tier labels can move the gap (3, 4, 6);
- that the original classifier agrees poorly with people (1);
- the conditional "no reasoning, no gap" result for Opus (paper §3.3.4);
- that compromise confounds the counts (App. E.2).

Not found anywhere:
1. An interval for the headline gap that resamples **queries**, or a count of how many queries
   carry it.
2. Whether the **same queries** carry the gap across prompt variants and models.
3. The "which models show a gap" comparison on the **odds scale**, with **power** given each
   model's base rate. Sheshadri et al. note that o-series models refuse nearly everything, but
   draw no statistical consequence for the count.
4. A count of the **routes** into "complied" by tier (PM score vs. instruction-following label vs.
   output surface).
5. Whether the released alignment-faking label is reproducible from **vocabulary**, with a held-out
   half.
6. The **spread** of the gap over all released variants, as against the default and the median
   paraphrase.
