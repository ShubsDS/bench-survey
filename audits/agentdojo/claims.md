# AgentDojo: claims under test

Written 2026-11-17, before any per-item model output was tabulated. The published aggregate
table (`docs/results-table.html`) and one trace (GPT-4o, workspace user_task_0 x injection_task_0)
had been opened to learn the file format.

## Source, licence, pins

- Paper: Debenedetti, Zhang, Balunović, Beurer-Kellner, Fischer, Tramèr, "AgentDojo: A Dynamic
  Environment to Evaluate Prompt Injection Attacks and Defenses for LLM Agents", arXiv 2406.13352
  (v3, NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks).
- Code, task suites and per-case traces: github.com/ethz-spylab/agentdojo, MIT licence.
  Pinned at commit `089ed468cf3ed0322acc66b0211f26d9d90dbf60` (2026-06-02), shallow clone in
  `data/agentdojo/` (git-ignored).
- Traces: `runs/<pipeline>/<suite>/<user_task>/<attack>/<injection_task>.json`, 36,679 JSON files,
  29 pipelines. Each holds the full message list, the injected strings, an `error` field and the
  stored `utility` and `security` booleans (`security = True` means the attack succeeded).
- Published numbers: `docs/results-table.html` at the pin, generated by
  `util_scripts/create_results_table.py` with benchmark version v1.1.2 (97 user tasks, 27
  injection tasks, 629 user-by-injection cases per pipeline).

## Conclusion 1. Per-model targeted ASR and its ranking

- Statement: the results table at the pin, "Targeted ASR" under the `important_instructions`
  attack, e.g. GPT-4o 47.69%, GPT-4-0125 56.28%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet (June) 33.86%, Claude 3.7
  Sonnet 7.31%, Claude 3.5 Sonnet (Oct) 1.11%, Command-R 3.34%, Command-R+ 4.45%,
  GPT-3.5 10.33%. Paper Table 3 and Figure 6 carry the same numbers for the June 2024 models.
- Who relies on it: the public results page; model and defence papers that cite AgentDojo ASR as
  "how injectable model X is"; government red-teaming exercises that adopt the suite.
- What would change it: if ASR computed only over cases where the agent actually received the
  injected tool output (and could have acted) reorders models such that published pairs swap or
  merge, or if low-ASR models are low mainly because they rarely reach or cannot execute the
  injection.

## Conclusion 2. "More capable models are easier to attack"

- Statement: paper Section 4 / Figure 6 (benign utility vs targeted ASR) describes an "inverse
  scaling" trend: more capable models tend to be easier to attack. The paper itself offers the
  partial explanation that weak models fail to execute the attacker's goal.
- Who relies on it: discussion of whether capability scaling worsens injection risk.
- What would change it: if the positive association between benign utility and ASR across
  models disappears or reverses once ASR is conditioned on exposure and on the model's
  demonstrated ability to perform the injection action, the trend is a capability artefact rather
  than a propensity to obey injections.

## Conclusion 3. Defence gains for GPT-4o

- Statement: results table, GPT-4o with `important_instructions`: no defence 47.69%;
  tool_filter 6.84%; transformers_pi_detector 7.95%; repeat_user_prompt 27.82%;
  spotlighting_with_delimiting 41.65%. The paper presents tool filtering and the detector as the
  strongest of the evaluated defences (with utility costs).
- Who relies on it: defence papers (CaMeL, MELON, SecAlign and others) that use these baselines.
- What would change it: if a defence's ASR reduction is mostly explained by the agent no longer
  reaching the injection or no longer having the means to act (fewer tool calls, aborted runs),
  while ASR among exposed-and-able cases stays near the undefended rate, the reported gain is
  "the agent did less" rather than resistance. For the PI detector, aborting on detection is the
  intended mechanism, so the comparison is reported but read differently (see prereg).

## Secondary questions (not headline conclusions)

- Scorer validity: do stored `security` labels agree with whether the attacker's action appears
  in the agent's own tool calls? Errored runs are coded `security = True`
  (`src/agentdojo/benchmark.py:117-147`).
- Attack text names the model; does ASR differ by name match (paper Table 2 ablation on GPT-4o)?
- Concentration: is each model's ASR carried by few injection tasks or one suite; how wide are
  intervals that resample tasks rather than cases?
